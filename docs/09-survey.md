# 09. Survey: "Where is the hidden source?"

Source: `src/modes/survey.rs` (rules), `src/field.rs::Term::Gauss` (the hidden source), `src/validate_survey.rs` (analysis), `src/app.rs::survey_frame` and `src/render.rs::{draw_zone_frame, draw_chevrons, draw_estimate, draw_source}` (interface). Levels `sur-1` to `sur-5`. Generated numbers: [`SURVEY_VALIDATION.md`](../SURVEY_VALIDATION.md).

**Status: prototype.** One level, validated against an *ideal* solver. No human has played it, and whether players choose scouts for what they can teach (rather than scattering them) is the open question. See section 10.

## 1. At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Decision | where to release each scout (any point in the zone), then where to put one estimate marker |
| When | scouts: one at a time, each seen to fly before the next is placed; the commit: once, last |
| Feedback | each scout's trail (5 s, shown in full, with direction chevrons) |
| Resources | **1 free scout** (flown at the start of every attempt) + `scouts` paid scouts (2 in `sur-1`) + **one** commit |
| Attempts | unlimited; a retry is only possible after a commit and hides the source somewhere else |
| Win | the committed marker is within `tol` (0.75) of the hidden source |
| Fixed | the charted background field, the source's strength `k` (1.6) and width `sigma` (1.0), the trail length, the free scout's start |
| Hidden | the source's position (and so everything its push does to the field) |

## 2. Information

The field in the level data is the **charted background**: a steady stream, `(1, 0)` everywhere, drawn as ordinary arrows (`G` changes their density, `K` makes them high-contrast). The real field is that stream plus one hidden local repeller (`Term::Gauss`) at an unknown position. The arrows never include it.

Visible at all times: the arrows, the dashed frame of the zone, the free scout's start (`F`) and trail, every scout placed so far (a numbered start point and its full trail with direction chevrons), the estimate marker with a ring showing the commit tolerance, the scouts left, the attempt number.

**Never shown before the commit:** the source position, its effect on the field, the outcome of any scout that has not been placed. A scout is only simulated when the player places it (`SurveyGame::place_scout`); there is no preview of a scout's real trail (level 5 adds a preview of its *charted* path, which depends on nothing hidden; section 9, level 5). `Round::revealed_source()` returns `None` until the round is committed, and the interface only reads the source through it.

**Speed.** The chevrons are spaced by *distance along the path* (every 1.4 world units), not by time, so they show direction without showing where the scout sped up. Speed is still partly visible: every trail lasts the same 5 s, so its length is its mean speed times 5.

**Push arrows.** Along each trail the interface draws small arrows every 0.3 s wherever the scout felt at least `PUSH_MIN` = 0.2 of extra push: the scout's measured velocity (`SurveySpec::push_samples`, a centred difference over +-0.1 s) minus the charted flow at that point. That extra term is the hidden source's own push, which points straight away from it (and is longer closer in, until the source's core), so lines drawn back along the arrows meet at the source. It depends on the trail and the charted field only, never the hidden position. A legend under the field explains it. Hovering the field with a tool active shows a faint ghost of what a click would place (a scout's start point, or the marker with its tolerance ring, or a red cross where a scout would be refused); it never shows a trail, except that level 5 also draws the scout's *charted* path (see level 5).

*The sensing range is the difficulty knob.* A scout shows arrows only where the push is at least `PUSH_MIN`, 2.43 units from the source (strength 1.6, sigma 1). Simulated with a 12 degree reading error, success within 0.75 by how the evidence was gathered:

| `PUSH_MIN` | free scout alone | + 1 scout placed blindly | + 2 scouts placed blindly | + 2 scouts within 1.8 of the source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.12 (first value) | 56% (4 of 6 positions solved at 80-88%) | 66% | 71% | 92% |
| **0.2 (shipped)** | **0%** | 18% | 36% | 90% |

At 0.12 most of the level was solved by the free scout, so paid scouts were redundant: the "fit-dominant" failure. At 0.2 the free scout rules out nothing but a band near its own line (no arrows is itself evidence: the source is more than about 2.4 from y = 0), a blind scout rarely helps, and a scout sent toward where the source must be does. The question the player faces is where to send the next scout. Test `a_scout_near_the_source_locates_it_and_the_free_scout_alone_never_does`. Not yet measured: how well the Test estimate overlay can locate a source from the free trail alone; the arrows cannot, and the overlay should be checked the same way before the level is called final.

*Why the arrows are local and not the end-point displacement.* A first version drew, for each trail, a ring where the scout would have ended with no source and a dashed arrow from it to the real end. It looked right and was not usable: measured against an ideal fit (which recovers all three of a player's missed attempts to 0.07), triangulating the end displacements gives a median error of 1.99 and 1% within 0.5, because a trail integrates the push along a curving path, so its net displacement does not point at the source. Players' misses (1.69, 1.30, 0.93) matched what that readout supports. The local push locates the source exactly (`a_scout_near_the_source_locates_it_and_the_free_scout_alone_never_does`). With a per-trail reading error of 8 / 15 / 25 degrees, simulated success within 0.75 is about 84-91 / 64-73 / 42-43 %, within 0.5 about 71-81 / 47-51 / 25 %: this is why the tolerance is 0.75.

**Test estimate (a budget).** With a marker placed, `T` or the **Test estimate** button flies every scout placed so far again *as if the source were at the marker* (`SurveyGame::test_estimate`) and shows the result as thin dashed trails next to the solid ones, with a small diamond marking which estimate they belong to. There are `TESTS_PER_ROUND` = 3 per attempt, refilled on retry; testing the same marker with the same scouts again is free (it would show the same thing), and a new scout makes the old result stale. The result is a snapshot: moving the marker does not update it. A test is a pure function of the marker and the scouts' start points, never of the hidden position (`a_test_needs_a_marker_and_never_reads_the_hidden_source` runs two rounds with different hidden positions and compares), so it checks a guess against evidence the scouts already gave and adds none.

The budget is a design choice with a stated cost. An unlimited, live overlay turns the level into sliding the marker until two curves match, which makes the scouts matter only when the free trail says too little and replaces the question "where should I send the next scout?" with "where should I drag the marker?". A budget makes *which estimate to test* a decision. The downside is that it borders on Experiment's loop (form a hypothesis, spend a trial, read the result); if Survey is meant to stay distinct from Experiment, the alternatives are showing only an end-point or a mismatch number per test, or dropping the overlay. Not playtested.

**Rays (reading aid, per level).** With `rays` on (`Rules::Survey`, `SurveySpec::push_rays`), every second push arrow also gets a long faint line running back along its own direction, toward the source. Lines from one trail, or from several, cross at the source, so the answer can be read off without extending arrows by eye (`rays_run_back_along_the_push_through_the_source`: each ray passes within 0.02 of the source). Search is unchanged: a scout still has to pass within its sensing range (`PUSH_MIN`) before there is anything to extend, and the free scout alone still locates none of the six positions. Level 1 is the teaching level and has rays on; `sur-3` has them off, so the player extends the arrows by eye. They are off there because a first version kept them on (the roadmap's aids accumulate) and a screenshot of play showed the effect: one scout in range, the rays met at a point, the marker went there. That is Level 1 again, and it made the level's one new idea (where to send the next scout when a trail only gives a line or nothing) cost nothing. With the rays off and a rougher reading model (12 degrees of bias per trail, 10 of jitter per arrow) a well-placed scout still solved 90-93% of the grazes and head-ons (49-72% of the quiet ones), blind pairs averaged 13-39%, and the best pair was always 100%; with the rays on, informed placement was 98-100%. (Those figures are from the repeller-only first version of the level; the figures with all the kinds are in the level's own section.)

After the commit: the source is drawn (a disc with eight rays, ringed at `sigma`), with a line from the marker to it and the text "The source was at (x, y)".

## 4. State and loop

```
Scouting --place_scout (while budget)--> Scouting
Scouting --set_marker--> Scouting (marker moves freely)
Scouting --commit (needs a marker)--> Committed
Committed --retry--> Scouting (next hidden position, only the free scout flown)
```

| State | Accepts | Rejects |
|---|---|---|
| `Scouting` (`verdict == None`) | `place_scout`, `set_marker`, `commit` | `retry` -> `NotFinished` |
| `Committed` (`verdict == Some`) | `retry` | `place_scout` -> `Finished`, `set_marker` / `commit` -> `Finished` |

`retry` is refused mid-round on purpose: abandoning a round would refund its scouts and allow unlimited scouting.

## 5. Exact rules

**The hidden source.** `Term::Gauss { c, k, sigma }` adds `v = k d exp(-|d|^2 / (2 sigma^2))` with `d = p - c`, a radial push away from `c` for `k > 0`. It is zero at the centre, peaks at distance `sigma` with speed `k sigma e^(-1/2)`, and falls off fast: 45% of the peak at 2 sigma, 5.5% at 3 sigma, 0.22% at 4 sigma, 0.003% at 5 sigma. With `k = 1.6`, `sigma = 1` the peak is 0.97, close to the background speed 1.0. It is the reason the mechanic works: `Term::Radial` has a 1/r tail that bends every scout a little wherever it is, so one scout already pins the source down (see the design evidence at the end of section 10). A local source can only be detected by passing near it.

**A scout.** `SurveySpec::fly(source, start)` simulates the charted field plus the hidden source from `start` at clock 0, with the level's bounds, no regions, and the level's time limit (`max_time = 5 s`) as the trail length. A trail ends early only by leaving the bounds. It is deterministic, and independent of every other scout (tested).

**Placing a scout** (`place_scout`), in this order: round committed -> `Finished`; no paid scouts left -> `NoScouts`; start outside the zone -> `OutOfZone`; within `MIN_SCOUT_SEPARATION` (0.05) of an earlier scout's start, the free one included -> `Duplicate` (it would repeat that trail). A rejected placement changes nothing. The zone is the rectangle x in [-5.5, 5.5], y in [-3.5, 3.5].

**The marker** (`set_marker`) is clamped into the zone and can be moved any number of times until the commit.

**The commit** (`commit`) needs a marker. `Verdict { distance, ok }` with `distance = |marker - source|` and `ok = distance <= tol` (inclusive). `won` becomes true on the first `ok` and stays true.

**The hidden position of each attempt** (`SurveySpec::source_for_attempt`): the level lists `pool` (6 authored positions) **in the order a player should meet them**. The first pass uses the pool in that order (level 1 opens with two grazes that show arrows at once; level 3 interleaves graze, head-on, quiet; the older rule "non-decreasing in candidates left after the free scout" is tested on level 1's original positions only). Every later pass is a seeded Fisher-Yates permutation of the pool (one per pass, from `level.seed`), and never opens on the position the previous pass ended on. So each position is used once per pass, no attempt repeats the one before, and the same level always gives the same sequence. A retry cannot be won by remembering the previous answer, and neither can a *different* attempt: a test requires every pair of positions in levels 1 and 3 to be more than twice the commit tolerance apart (so an earlier answer cannot be re-marked), and in the kinds level (whose sources cluster within reach of the free scout) every pair of the same kind to be more than the tolerance apart, so only the kind is left to read. Before this, one seeded order was used from the start, and it put the two hardest positions (130 candidates left after the free scout) first and second.

**The free scout** is flown at the start of every attempt from `free` (-5, 0). It travels right with the stream for 5 s, so it only reaches x = 0: a source in the right half of the field leaves it untouched.

## 6. Success and failure

Success: the commit is within 0.75 of the source. Failure: any other commit; the source is revealed, the player may retry. There is no other failure state. The level is complete after the first success; further attempts are allowed.

## 7. Invariants and edge cases

- The source is unreadable before the commit (`revealed_source` is `None`); tested.
- Scouts are independent and deterministic: placing scout B never changes scout A's trail; tested.
- The free scout is not counted against the budget and is never removable.
- A source never brings a scout to rest (a repeller pushes away), so no single scout can come to rest on it and give its position away. Tested over every lattice scout and every authored position (`no_scout_ever_comes_to_rest...`). An *attracting* source would not be safe in general: `Outcome::Stalled` at the source is a free answer.
- A won level stays won when a later attempt is missed.
- Positions in the pool are inside the zone, at least 1.5 apart, and off the validator's 0.5 candidate grid (tested), so the validator cannot flatter itself.
- Only the free scout's start is fixed; a paid scout placed exactly there is `Duplicate`.

## 8. Feedback

Messages (`app.rs::survey_place`, `survey_commit`):

| Event | Message |
|---|---|
| hit | "Found it: your marker is 0.31 from the source (within 0.75)." (green; level-complete banner) |
| miss | "Missed: your marker is 1.42 from the source (needs within 0.75). Retry (R) hides it somewhere else." (red) |
| commit without a marker | "Place the marker first (M, then click)." |
| no scouts left | "No scouts left. Use the marker (M) to say where the source is." |
| outside the zone | "Place scouts inside the dashed frame." |
| on top of a scout | "A scout is already there: it would draw the same trail." |

Colours and shapes: the free scout is light grey and labelled `F`; paid scouts use the shared run palette and are labelled `1`, `2`; the estimate is a violet diamond with a tolerance ring; the revealed source is a vermillion disc with rays. Scout identity does not rely on colour alone (labels, start squares).

Hints (`H`, in order), in plain language: (1) the grey arrows are the flow with nothing hidden, so a path that bends away from them is being pushed by something hidden; (2) the small arrows on a trail show that push and point away from the source, so extend them backwards or follow the faint lines to where they cross; (3) a scout only feels the source from about 2.4 away, so if the first ones felt nothing, release the next nearer to where you suspect it. None gives the answer. `sur-3` chooses its hints by what the free scout showed (nothing, one arrow, or several: `hint_texts` in `src/app.rs`), because the three situations call for different advice; its static hints are only a fallback. The first version of those hints talked about arrows "on a line" and a scout that "repeats the answer", and a player who had already solved the level by extending the arrows found them unintelligible.

## 9. Levels

| Level | Configuration | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| `sur-1` Something in the Stream | uniform stream (1, 0); source k = 1.6, sigma = 1; 1 free + 2 paid scouts; 5 s trails; tolerance 0.75; 6 authored positions | first contact: learn that a trail only speaks for the places it went near |

**Current positions.** In play order: (-2.4, 2.0) and (-1.5, -1.9), two **grazes** about 2 from the free scout's path, where it feels the source and shows 5 arrows (and the rays) from the first attempt, then (-3.25, -2.25), (-0.25, 2.25), (3.25, -1.75) and (2.25, 2.25), out of its reach (no arrows: a search). Under the arrow-reading model (`SURVEY_VALIDATION.md`) the free scout alone solves the two grazes 2-5% of the time (0% by eye), scouts released 1 above and below solve them 92%, blind pairs average 15-41%, and the best pair is always 100%. The level is complete after one find, so a player who reads the first attempt well never meets the searches. This replaces the original six positions, in which the free scout showed **no arrow on any attempt**, so a player's first contact with a source was a blind placement that worked about 10-29% of the time (a reviewer's point, and it matched a player needing two attempts).

**Original positions (legacy).** The six positions the level first shipped with, in play order (easy to hard), were (-3.25, -2.25), (-2.75, 2.25), (-0.75, -2.25), (-0.25, 2.25), (3.25, -1.75), (2.25, 2.25), kept in `levels::SURVEY_LEGACY_POOL` for the older validator and its tests. They were chosen from 140 candidate positions on a 0.5 lattice offset by (0.25, 0.25): 63 pass the validator's requirements (solvable at both precisions, free scout alone does not settle it, still ambiguous afterwards). Of those, **55 are settled by the adaptive player with one paid scout and only 8 need two, and all 8 lie in one small patch** (x in [2.25, 3.25], y in [1.25, 2.25]), where the free trail sees nothing. The pool takes one of them, (2.25, 2.25). Measured per position and over 300 random sources: [`SURVEY_VALIDATION.md`](../SURVEY_VALIDATION.md).

### Level 2: `sur-2` Which Way It Pushes

The new demand is the **kind** of source. Each attempt hides a repeller, an attractor or a swirl (counter-clockwise or clockwise), and the marker carries a kind too (**Y** or the *Marker kind* button cycles repeller, attractor, swirl CCW, swirl CW); a commit is found only if the marker is within tolerance **and** names the right kind (`Verdict.kind_ok`, `SurveySpec::judge_typed`). Sigma 1, strength 1.3 (peak push 0.79, below the stream speed), tolerance 0.75, 1 free + 2 paid scouts, 3 tests, rays on. The kind is a property of the attempt (`Rules::Survey.kinds`, parallel to `pool`; `kind_for_attempt`), and it travels with its position in every pass. Test estimate flies the scouts again for the *chosen* kind, so a wrong-kind marker shows wrong dashes.

**Completion and attempts.** The level is complete when a **repeller, an attractor and a swirl** have each been found (`SurveyGame::complete`, `found`); until then a hit is progress, not the end (no banner; the message says "1 of 3 kinds found. Press R for the next" and the panel shows three boxes that fill in as kinds are found). Each attempt hides the first of those three kinds not yet found, taking that kind's positions in order (`pick_index`), so a miss is followed by another of the same kind and a player who finds each first time plays exactly three attempts: repeller, attractor, swirl. Positions: repeller (-2.5, 1.2) then (-2.6, -0.9); attractor (-1.5, -1.3) then (-2.2, 1.4); swirl CCW (-3.0, 1.1), swirl CW (-1.0, -1.2), and a hard swirl close to the free scout's path (-1.8, 0.4). After the level is complete, "Keep playing" serves the pool in authored order. This replaced a first version in which the first hit of any kind ended the level, which let a player finish it without ever meeting an attractor or a swirl (a player pointed this out).

*Reading rule.* For a repeller or an attractor the arrows lie along lines through the source, so the faint lines (drawn here as whole lines, since an attractor's arrows point the other way) meet at it; whether the arrows point away from the crossing or toward it says which. A swirl's arrows are at right angles to the line from its centre, so their lines never meet; **P** switches the faint lines from "along each arrow" to "at right angles to each arrow" (one family at a time, so the two never overlay), and the right-angle lines meet at a swirl's centre. Each line is drawn as strongly as its arrow, so the long arrows near the source speak loudest and weak ones far from any source barely show. After a miss on a swirl the game says why the lines never crossed and to press P. A picture key under the field shows the three kinds (a centre with arrows going out, in, and around, each with its words), and after every commit a ring of white arrows is drawn around the revealed source showing what that kind really does, so even a miss shows how a repeller, an attractor or a swirl looks.

*Why P and not N.* The roadmap names the overlay N, but N is "Next" on the level-complete banner.

*What was checked.* The free scout alone gives both the kind and the place at every one of the seven positions, read with 8 degrees of bias per trail and 6 of jitter per arrow: at least 85% right (a test), 92-100% as measured, because every source sits within about 1.2 of its path. The distance matters: scanning repeller positions, those 0.9-1.1 off the path read 92-100% across x = -3.4 to -1.4, those 1.3 off read 60-90%, and a second repeller first tried at (-3.5, -1.4), near the scout's start where the arrows cover only one side, read 45% and was moved. This is deliberate: the level is a **reading level** (the roadmap's term): its new demand is telling the kinds apart, and the decisions about where to send scouts are `sur-3`'s. Lines along the arrows meet within 0.1 of a repeller's or attractor's position and do not meet for a swirl; right-angle lines do the reverse (`level_two_lines_along_the_arrows_meet...`). A marker of the wrong kind placed exactly on the source misses the free scout's trail by more than 0.15 for every attempt and every wrong kind (`level_two_a_marker_of_the_wrong_kind_never_matches_the_dashes_however_close`).

*What the first version got wrong (from a screenshot of play).* The legend under the field still said the arrows "point away from the source", which is only true of a repeller; nothing showed what the three kinds look like, though the whole level rests on telling them apart; both families of lines were drawn at once, as strong for a weak arrow far from any source as for a strong one beside it, so the crossing was lost in a web of lines; and a miss message of about 190 characters ran under the Hint and Retry buttons. The player's verdict was that the level was too hard and the logic unclear. The key, the single family of lines weighted by strength, the post-commit ring and a message of at most 140 characters (tested) are the response. None of this has been tried by a person since.

Not validated: that the trap works (a person reading a swirl with ordinary lines and expecting them to cross), whether the attractor/repeller distinction is visible enough in the arrows, and the old trail-similarity validator, which models a repeller only and is not applied to this level.

*Order.* The two levels were first built the other way round (`sur-2` One Look, `sur-3` Which Way) so that existing progress would not move; a player suggested the swap and it is the roadmap's own order: first learn what the three kinds look like and how to read them (a reading level where the free scout is enough), then learn where to send scouts when a trail gives only a line or nothing. Level ids follow the order, so a save made under the old order has the two levels' completion swapped.

### Level 3: `sur-3` One Look Is a Line

Same stream and arena, now with **all the kinds of source from level 2** (a repeller, an attractor or a swirl, each attempt), narrower and weaker: sigma 0.8, strength 1.65 (peak push 0.80, so nothing stalls), commit tolerance 0.6 (0.75 sigma), felt out to 1.87 at `PUSH_MIN`. 1 free + 2 paid scouts, 3 tests, the **cleared band** on, the **rays off** and no P overlay (`Rules::Survey.band`, `.rays`): the player extends the arrows by eye, and the picture key under the field still shows what each kind looks like. As in level 2 the marker carries a kind (Y) and a commit needs the kind as well as the place. The level is complete after **three attempts found** (`SurveyGoal::Finds(3)`, shown as "Attempt N - found k of 3"), not one of each kind: the point here is where to send scouts, and the kinds only add to what a trail can mean.

Six hidden positions and kinds, in play order, interleaved so the three attempts it takes to finish are one of each situation and, as it happens, one of each kind: graze repeller (-1.5, 1.6), head-on attractor (1.55, 0.05), quiet swirl CCW (3, 3), then graze swirl CW (0.5, -1.6), graze repeller (0.4, 1.5), quiet attractor (2.5, -3.3). (The first version of the level was repeller-only and ordered graze, head-on, graze, head-on, quiet, quiet, with a second head-on 0.18 from the first; a player pointed out that, after the kinds level, this one should use the kinds too.) The free scout flies y = 0 from x = -5 and ends at x = 0.

*The three attempt types.* **Graze**: the source is at the edge of the free scout's reach, so it shows one to three weak arrows (a thin wedge of possible sources). **Head-on** (one attempt): on the free scout's own line, just beyond where its trail ends, so it shows one weak arrow and the answer is "on this line" (along the arrow for a repeller or attractor, at right angles to it for a swirl, so one arrow does not even say which). **Quiet**: out of reach, no arrows at all. Each is checked by `level_three_attempts_are_graze_head_on_quiet_graze_graze_quiet`.

*The cleared band* (`SurveySpec::cleared`). Where a scout felt less than `PUSH_MIN`, a source within the sensing radius (1.87, less a 0.15 margin) would have pushed it harder, so the source is not there: the band is the union of those discs, shaded under the field. A silent scout therefore still rules ground out, and the shading shows the player what it ruled out. Two details matter for soundness. A spot clears only if the spots just before and after it were also quiet: the push also vanishes at the source's own centre, so a scout that passes straight through a source feels almost nothing right there, and without this rule its band would cover the source (a test found exactly that). And the band depends only on the trail and the known strength and width. `the_cleared_band_never_covers_the_hidden_source` checks every authored position of both levels against every lattice start.

**What was measured (kind and place both have to be right; arrows read with an 8 degree bias per trail and 6 degrees of jitter per arrow, "by eye" 12 and 10).** The free scout alone settles none of the six (at most 5% at the final positions; the first swirl graze, (0.5, -1.4), could be read from it 22-24% of the time and was moved to (0.5, -1.6): 2 arrows, 0%). A second and third scout released 1 unit above and below the line solve the grazes and head-ons (93-100%) but not the quiet ones (0%); 2 above and below solve the quiet ones (87-100%). Scouts released where the free scout was repeat its look on the head-on and quiet attempts (0-1%). Blind pairs on the whole lattice average 24-56% against 100% for the best pair, so where the scouts go decides the outcome. Read by eye, well-placed scouts solve 72-100% of the grazes and head-ons and 57-99% of the quiet ones. These are the roadmap's three conditions for the level (free scout never settles it, every attempt solvable in two scouts, a same-line scout cannot work), measured on the arrows the player actually sees, not on the older trail-similarity validator (`SURVEY_VALIDATION.md`), which models repellers only and is not applied to this level.

**Findings that changed the design from the roadmap.**
1. *Head-on only means "line only" beyond about 1.5 from the end of the free trail.* A source inside that range slows the free scout by 0.2-0.5 units at its end, and moving the source 0.6 along the line moves that end by 0.19-0.33: visible in the Test estimate dashes. With three tests a player could slide a marker along the line and settle it with the free scout alone. At 1.5 and beyond the shift is 0.12 or less and one arrow remains. (Positions on the line *inside* the free scout's reach are settled by the free scout anyway: the arrows reverse direction at the source.)
2. *"Look from a different direction" is not available.* In a uniform stream every scout flies the same way. What varies is height above or below the line, and how far along it the scout starts. Hint 2 says "a different height". Direction becomes a real choice only where the background circulates.
3. *"A second scout on the same line adds nothing" is only true for a scout released where the free scout was.* One released farther along the line sees farther and can help on a graze. That is a longer look from the same side, not a second viewpoint, but it is not nothing.
4. *An attractor pulls a passing scout toward it*, so a scout released 1 off the line could feel the quiet attractor first placed at (2.5, -3.0) (17% of the time, enough to break "a quiet source cannot be felt from 1 off the line"); it sits at (2.5, -3.3).
6. *Two head-ons do not fit.* A head-on source must sit 1.4-1.6 beyond the free trail's end, so a second one was 0.18 from the first and well inside the tolerance: a player could re-mark the earlier answer and pass without scouting (a reviewer's point). A second head-on would need a different free scout line per attempt, or positions more than 1.2 apart, neither of which exists here, so the level has one head-on and a third graze, and `no_two_hidden_positions_in_a_level_are_within_twice_the_tolerance` keeps it that way.
5. *The reading model needs an honest rule for near-parallel lines.* Nobody can extend lines that run within about 11 degrees of each other to a crossing, so the test estimator refuses them (`nearest_to_lines`). An earlier version of the experiment used a cutoff that depended on the arrows' strength, which discarded weak-arrow reads and made one position look unsolvable-from-the-free-scout (5%) when a scale-free rule says about 22%.

The fixed pattern at height +-2 (x = -3) solves the grazes because they sit about 1.5 from the line, and fails the head-on and quiet attempts: it does not fail the grazes, which the roadmap expected. Not validated: how readable the shading and the arrows are to a person, how hard telling the kind is when the arrows are few (a single head-on arrow cannot say), whether three tests make the level easier than intended, and whether the quiet attempts feel like a search or like guessing.

### Level 4: `sur-4` Two of Them

**Two sources are hidden at once** (`Rules::Survey.partners`: a second source of the same kind hidden with each pool entry), identical repellers of level 3's strength (sigma 0.8, strength 1.65), and the player has **two markers**. The Marker tool places A on the first click and B on the second; after that a click moves the nearer marker (`SurveyGame::place_marker`). A commit needs both and is **found when each marker is within the tolerance (0.6) of a different source, in either assignment** (`SurveySpec::judge_pair`; the sources are identical, so the order does not matter); the verdict's distance is the worse marker's. Test estimate flies the scouts with both markers as sources and also needs both. 1 free + 3 paid scouts, **4 tests** (one more than the single-source levels: `SurveyGame::tests_per_round`), **short guide lines** (stubs 1.2 long, `PAIR_RAY_LEN`) and no cleared band. The roadmap's reason for dropping the band is that overlapping pushes cancel near the midpoint, so silence proves less. The rays are on because the roadmap's own account of the trap needs them ("the rays converge there"): from a scout beside one source they meet at that source, from far away they meet at the midpoint, which is what makes two look like one. The first build had them off and a player called the level too hard (he had one marker 0.2 from a source and the other 0.81, with the tolerance 0.6). The level is complete after **four finds** (`SurveyGoal::Finds(4)`), which reaches the 2.8 separation and its trap.

*Attempts*, in order of separation: 4.5 and 4.0 (one source above the free scout's line and one below, so the free scout grazes both), then side by side along the stream at 3.2, 2.8, 2.4 and 2.2, 1.5 to 1.6 above or below the free scout's line. Pairs, first and second source: (-3.7, 1.7) (-0.75, -1.7); (-0.3, 1.7) (-2.4, -1.7); (-0.3, 1.6) (-3.5, 1.6); (0.2, -1.6) (-2.6, -1.6); (0.3, 1.5) (-2.1, 1.5); (-1.9, -1.5) (-4.1, -1.5). The first two teach the two-marker commit; the rest carry the trap. **Every pair lies within the free scout's first reach, so it always has a lead** (`level_four_the_free_scout_always_gives_a_lead_on_the_pair`): it comes within about 2 of both sources and shows 2 to 5 arrows. The first layout scattered pairs over the whole arena, and in play (nine screenshots of one session) the attempts went like this: where a scout happened to feel both sources the pair was found (worst marker 0.2 to 0.3 off); where it never felt the second source (attempt 1: the upper source behind the scouts' starts; attempt 5: the near source held one scout up and pushed the other away) marker B was a blind guess 3.3 and 7.7 off, and the player's verdict was "sometimes it is clear enough, sometimes it is just: what should I do?". Measured on that layout, the free scout felt at most one source in every attempt and neither in three of the six. The arena is 11 by 7 and a scout only feels about 1.9 either side of a 5-long path, so three scouts cannot sweep it: without a lead the second source was luck.

*The trap (the ghost).* From beyond their separation two sources look like one in the middle, and **both markers on the midpoint** give the push of one source of double strength. The roadmap had not checked how visible that mismatch is; measured: for sources **side by side along the stream**, scouts that pass to either side barely feel either source, and the ghost's trail is within 0.14 of the real one at every separation from 1.6 to 4.5 (`level_four_two_sources_seen_from_the_side...`). A scout flying **along the line between them** separates the ghost clearly (a trail deviation of 0.5 to 0.7 at separations of 2.2 to 2.8), as does one that passes close to one source. With the sources one above the other the ghost does not fit even the side scouts (0.4 to 0.7), so the first two attempts have no trap. After a miss with a marker nearer the midpoint than to either source (and not within the tolerance of either) the game says: "A marker sat between the two sources: from far away, two look like one." (`Round::marker_between`).

*Identifiability* (`validate_survey::pair_report`, the table in `SURVEY_VALIDATION.md`; hypotheses are pairs of points on a 1-unit lattice, consistent when every scout's trail stays within 0.35 of the real one). The free scout alone leaves 34 to 1,481 pairs open (the worst 3.9 to 9.9 units off). The naive scouts (the free scout and scouts released to either side) admit the ghost on all four side-by-side attempts and not on the first two. For every attempt there are **three paid scouts that, with the free scout, leave one or two lattice pairs consistent (0.4 to 1.2 from the truth, which is lattice resolution) and neither the ghost nor a near-ghost** (a lattice pair with its markers within 1.0 of each other: to a player the same mistake). Those sets always contain a scout that reaches the far source without passing the near one.

**Findings that changed the design.**
1. *A near source shadows a far one.* At separation 2.2 side by side on the free scout's own height, the head-on scout was held up before the first source (its push plus the tail of the second exceeds the stream speed) and the two side scouts were pushed away before reaching the second, so hypotheses with one correct source and the other anywhere stayed consistent (39 of them, up to 7 units off). A solving set therefore has to include a scout that reaches the far source on its own; the searched sets do.
2. *The free scout must not already fly along the pair's line.* Attempt 5 first sat at height -0.5, 0.5 from the free scout's line, so the free scout nearly flew along the axis and separated the two sources before the player had scouted. The side-by-side pairs are at least 1.4 above or below the free scout's line.
3. *The hint box hid the playfield.* With all three hints open (they stayed open on every later attempt, and the button then disabled itself) the box covered the lower left, where a trail and a source were. Hints now close on a new attempt, hide when the player acts, and toggle with H once all are out. The header text ("Attempt 3 - found 1 of 4") also overprinted the title, and the pair reveal text overlapped the miss message; both were layout bugs in the panel, fixed.
4. *Full guide lines made the level too easy, none made it too hard, so they are stubs.* With the whole-arena lines on, a player placed two scouts beside the pair and the lines crossed exactly on each source (both markers within 0.14); with none, he had one marker 0.81 out. A model-based reader (it knows the push profile and fits two sources to the arrow *directions*, with the lines' reading error 8/6 degrees, a stub's 10/8, none 12/10) says the aid itself moves success only 0-25 points when the scouts are well placed (attempt 3: 78%, 72%, 52%); where the scouts go matters far more (random scouts 7-61%). So the aid is not the dial that sets difficulty, but it decides how much the player has to *do* with an arrow: a full line is the answer drawn on the screen. A stub shows the arrow's direction accurately (it is short enough that the sources, 0.5 to 1.9 from the arrow, are not on it) and the player still extends it and judges where two would meet. The stub length is a guess between those two, not a measurement, and the reading errors assigned to each aid are assumptions.
5. *The search for a solving set must exclude the ghost explicitly.* The first search ranked sets by the worst consistent lattice pair; the ghost sits on the midpoint, which is not on the lattice, so for attempt 6 the "best" set left it consistent. The search now skips any set the ghost agrees with, and the test checks the ghost directly (`level_four_every_attempt_is_solvable...`).
6. *A lead on both sources and the ghost pull against each other.* Where the free scout feels both sources individually, the player can see two clusters and there is no ghost; where it feels neither, there is no lead. The layout settles it: the free scout comes within about 2 of each source (the sensing radius is 1.87, so the arrows are weak, 2 to 5 in all), the arrows radiate from the pair's region like those of one source in the middle (the stacked ghost deviates from the free trail by only 0.2 on the side-by-side pairs), and so the lead says "something is here, go and look" without saying whether it is one or two. For the first two attempts the free scout grazes a source above its line and a source below it, and the ghost is clearly wrong (deviation 2.5 to 3.4). An earlier fix moved attempt 6 off the top edge (trails run off the arena there); that attempt is now at (-1.9, -1.5) and (-4.1, -1.5) as part of this layout.
7. *The search for solving sets must exclude near-ghosts as well as the exact ghost* (found when attempt 6's chosen set left a nearly stacked pair consistent; the report's "ghost survives: no" had only checked the midpoint).
8. *Marker pairs must not be reusable.* Two side-by-side pairs 0.6 to 0.9 apart would let a player re-mark an earlier answer. Every two attempts are at least 1.4 apart in the better assignment (twice the tolerance is 1.2), tested in `level_four_no_two_attempts_can_reuse_a_pair_of_markers`.

Hints: (1) from far away two sources can look like one in the middle, and if both markers on one spot fit the dashes that may be what you are seeing; (2) a scout that passes close to one source feels it almost alone, one that flies along the line between them feels both in turn; (3) test your markers, and if no pair on one spot matches, pull them apart.

Not validated: how readable the arrows are for two sources (the single-source reading model does not apply, so the report skips the level in that section); whether four finds at three scouts each is too long (the knobs, if it is still too hard: `SurveyGoal::Finds`, the tolerance, or the number of tests; a tolerance of 0.8, the width of the orange ring, would need attempts 3 and 5 moved apart, since they are 1.4 apart now and the limit would be 1.6); whether four tests is the right number; whether people find the ghost convincing or just never stack their markers; whether the "between" message is understood; and the level has never been played.

### Level 5: `sur-5` The Gyre

**The background circulates**, so a scout does not fly straight and *where it starts* is no longer *where it looks*. The charted field is a counter-clockwise vortex about the centre of the arena (`Term::Vortex`, strength 4, core 1.5) plus a gentle inward drift (`Term::Radial`, strength -0.6): a scout spirals in at about 0.9-1.1 over the ground the hidden positions are on (the flow is slower, 0.65, only in the far corners, and zero at the exact centre, where a scout released stays and the preview shows no path). The source is level 3's repeller (sigma 0.8, strength 1.65, peak push 0.8, tolerance 0.6), alone, so nothing stalls anywhere a position is. 1 free + 2 paid scouts, 3 tests, **the cleared band on** (one source again, so silence is evidence), **guide lines off** (reading arrows is level 3's lesson; here it is the placing), complete after **three finds**.

**The new aid, the charted-path preview** (`Rules::Survey.preview`, `SurveySpec::fly_charted`, `app.rs::survey_preview`): while the Scout tool is active, a pale dashed line follows the cursor (or the keyboard cursor) showing where a scout released there would fly with nothing hidden, with a ring where it ends. It uses only the charted field, so it reveals nothing about the source (`the_charted_flight_does_not_depend_on_the_hidden_source`), and it is honest: a scout whose charted path stays 0.8 beyond the felt radius of the source (where the push is under 0.03) flies it to within 0.1 (`level_five_the_preview_is_the_real_trail...`). Where the preview and the real trail part, the source did it. The line is drawn only on levels that set the flag, and only for a position the click would accept.

*Positions*, in play order: (-3, 1), (2.5, -0.5), (0.5, 2), (1, -1.5), (-1.5, 2.5), (3.5, -1.5). The free scout is released at (-4, 0), flies down the left side and ends at (-0.9, -3.2). The first is a graze (two weak arrows from the free scout); the rest it never feels, and the second, fourth and sixth lie **on the free scout's own streamline, beyond where it stopped** (it flies five seconds; the flow goes on), so a scout released where it stopped carries on and feels them (7, 9 and 5 arrows; a careful reader with the free scout and that one scout gets the second right 72% of the time). The others are reached from elsewhere on the ring. Measured with the arrow-reading model (careful reading): the free scout alone is never read right, blind pairs of lattice scouts average 17-46% and a well-placed pair always solves it (`level_five_blind_placement_is_not_enough_but_a_good_pair_always_exists`); single curved trails can already be read (best single scout 100% for every position), because the arrows along a curving path are not collinear, which is unlike level 3. No two positions are within twice the tolerance.

**Findings that changed the design.**
1. *A launch strip was tried and dropped.* Restricting scouts to a left strip (so that "where can my scouts even go" was literal) reached only the lower-left of the arena: most strip releases reached the left half, none the right. The roadmap row asks for planning paths in a flowing background, not for a restricted zone, so the whole zone is open and the preview is the constraint made visible.
2. *"A scout released on the free scout's line adds nothing" is only true of the part already flown.* The first draft of the tests (and of hint 2) claimed a scout released part-way along the free trail sees nothing new. It does see new ground: the part beyond where the free scout stopped. Hint 2 now says the part already flown shows nothing new, and the level's second position uses exactly that.
3. *The preview is exact only well clear of the source.* The first tolerance (0.05 beyond the felt radius + 0.3) failed: the push at 2.2 from the source is still 0.08, and the gyre carries it. The test now asks for 0.1 beyond the felt radius + 0.8.

Hints: (1) the pale line is where a scout released at the cursor would fly with nothing hidden, and it only feels the source if that line passes within about 2; (2) the shaded ground is where a source would already have been felt, so release the next scout so its line crosses unshaded ground (the part already flown shows nothing new); (3) every scout is carried round the same way and the free scout stopped after five seconds: its pale line from where it stopped shows where the flow takes a scout next.

Not validated: whether the preview makes the level too easy (it shows the whole path before the scout is spent; the knobs: the number of paid scouts, the tolerance, or showing only the first part of the line); whether the circulating flow reads as a gyre to a person (the grey arrows show it); whether people use the shading together with the preview; and the level has never been played. The reading model (careful reader) is an assumption, not a measurement.

## 10. Distinctness and known weaknesses

**What it is meant to add.** Every other mechanic asks the player to *get a probe somewhere*, or to run tests on a *known* system. In Survey the scouts' own outcomes do not matter; they are instruments, and success is a statement about a hidden thing. The decision is *which observation to make*, under a budget, given what is already known.

**Against the project's own check** (`08-comparison.md`, section 5): Survey controls a release point (like Position), between flights (like Experiment's runs), and observes finished trails (like Experiment). By the letter of that check it is therefore **borderline**. What differs is the role of the decision (choosing evidence under a limited budget about an unknown *structure*, where Experiment's runs are unlimited and about a known system with an unknown *relationship*), and the check does not measure that. This is the weakest-supported claim here and only playtesting can decide it.

Known weaknesses, with numbers (300 random continuous sources; these come from the legacy trail-similarity section of `SURVEY_VALIDATION.md`, which is the ideal-solver view; the file leads with the arrow-reading model, which is what the player has):

1. **Evidence is from an ideal solver, not a person.** The "adaptive player" knows the candidate set and computes which scout splits it best. It shows the level *can* reward deliberate placement; it does not show that players reason that way. `adaptive_play` runs the same player through the real `SurveyGame` using only the trails it returns, and a test checks it agrees with the model.
2. **A sensible fixed spread is nearly as good.** At the coarse precision (what a person can use) the adaptive player succeeds on 81% of random sources, two scouts at a fixed pattern on 75%, random scouts on 64%; at the fine precision 96%, 92% and 83%. The lead over a fixed pattern is only 6 points (4 fine). With 3 scouts it is 9 (3). It is clear against random scattering (+17, +13). An earlier estimate of +17 over the fixed pattern came from 120 sources and was noise.
3. **The budget is slack for ideal play.** On the original positions the adaptive player needs one paid scout on 5 of 6; only (2.25, 2.25) needs both. For a player who does not know the answer it is a real constraint over random sources (one scout 73%, two 81%).
4. **Difficulty is mostly "did the free trail get near it?".** The free scout only reaches x = 0, and the hardest positions lie in the one patch it never sees. Easy positions are those the free trail already bends around.
5. **The free scout alone settles 39% of random sources (57% fine).** Authored positions are chosen where it does not (tested), but a *random* hidden position would often make the level trivial; that is why the pool is authored, and why any new level needs the validator.
6. **Retries cycle through only 6 positions.** A player who retries more than six times will see positions repeat (in a new order each pass).
7. **The estimate is a single point; strength and width are known.** Uncertainty about strength would make trails fit several locations for reasons unrelated to the intended lesson, so it is excluded from this prototype.
8. **The validator's candidate grid is its own device.** Candidates sit on a 0.5 grid; the game's solution space is continuous. Success in the validator means the centroid of the surviving candidates is within tolerance, a stand-in for a good human estimate.
9. **Trail length leaks mean speed** (section 2), and the 5 s window means a scout can only be informative about the 5 or so units it travels.
10. **The two precision thresholds are choices.** `EPS_FINE` = 0.15 (a difference a careful player could see) and `EPS_COARSE` = 0.4 (the precision of *mental* inference). The coarse one is a difficulty parameter to calibrate by playtest, and "maximum deviation along the trail" is a simple stand-in for what people notice. At the fine precision the level is much easier: the free scout alone settles 57% of random sources and a fixed pattern 92%.
11. **The tolerance is calibrated on a handful of misses, not a playtest.** The first value, 0.5, was a guess. Six consecutive misses by one player landed at 0.52, 0.55, 0.61, 0.66, 0.90 and 1.05 (found the region, not the point). 1.0 (= sigma) was tried and rejected: the free scout alone then fully settles (-2.75, 2.25), which the level forbids. 0.75 keeps all six positions valid; ideal-play success moves from 81% to 93% (coarse) and fixed pattern from 75% to 81%. The Test estimate overlay is the other half of the answer: it gives a person a way to reach precision that mental inference does not.

Why a local source, and why sigma = 1.0 (design evidence from a width scan: 120 sources, 3 scouts, coarse precision; results listed as adaptive / fixed pattern / random): a 1/r repeller made the free scout alone solve 99.5% of sources; a Gaussian with sigma = 1.2 gave 88 / 87 / 82 (almost no reason to place scouts carefully); sigma = 1.0 gave 89 / 72 / 66 (at 120 sources; see 2 above); sigma = 0.8 gave 82 / 63 / 54; sigma = 0.6 gave 71 / 38 / 37 but was too hard for a first level.

**How the validator works** (`src/validate_survey.rs`). Candidates for the source: 221 points on a 0.5 grid. A scout at lattice point `s` would draw a different trail under each candidate; two candidates are *indistinguishable from `s`* when their trails (34 samples, one per 0.15 s) never differ by more than `eps`. The evidence a scout gives is the set of candidates whose predicted trail matches the observed one (the best fits if none does). Players compared on the same sources: a **fixed pattern**, **random scouts**, an **adaptive** player (each scout chosen to minimise the expected number of remaining candidates), and an **oracle** that knows the truth and tries every combination of lattice scouts. A level is *solvable* iff the oracle wins; that is a hard requirement. *Non-triviality* is evaluated over a distribution of sources, not one.
